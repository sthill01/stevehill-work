Section 01 · The Agreement

Day 120 — what we agree this looks like.

Before any work begins, this is the agreement: by Day 120, IHS has a working marketing measurement system that produces four conditions. Every deliverable that follows is designed backwards from these.

01
Proper tracking setup
Every brand, every channel, every conversion event tagged with the right convention. UTM, GTM, ServiceTitan Campaign + Job Tag fields, call-source mapping. We can trust what we are measuring.
02
Systems communicating
Ad platforms, GA4 / GTM, CallRail, ServiceTitan, revenue, and reviews all reconcile through agreed data contracts. No more "my number doesn't match your number."
03
Reporting + dashboards
One portfolio scorecard plus role-specific Power BI views. Daily refresh on the pilot, weekly portfolio-wide. Every dashboard names a decision owner.
04
Leakage identification
When a metric drops, the system tells us WHERE: media, intake, booking, dispatch, field ops, billing, or follow-up. No more guessing whose problem it is.

Section 05 · Pilot, Then Replicate

Ten brands is too many to attack in parallel.

The plan runs one brand fully through the framework first, then cascades. Risk shrinks, trust compounds, governance gets negotiated with one GM not ten.

REASON 01

Risk shrinks

If a piece of the model breaks — taxonomy, closed-loop logic, Power BI refresh — we break it on one brand. Fix it once. Replicate the fixed version.

REASON 02

Trust compounds faster

One brand fully landed by day 90 is more credible than ten brands half-built. The pilot becomes the proof object the other GMs see before committing.

REASON 03

Politics gets easier

Negotiating shared governance with one GM is a conversation. With ten simultaneously it's a committee. The pilot sets the precedent.

REASON 04

IT lift right-sized

One ServiceTitan configuration to wire up first. One CallRail integration. Cascading is known-pattern application — not exponential build-out.

Inventory + Pilot Selection

Section 09 · Discovery Framework

Observe before changing. Respect what's working.

Each brand gets the same six-dimension audit: tech stack, data & tracking, knowledge base, process preferences, decision owners, history & sentiment. Every observation gets knowns/unknowns scoring and a four-bucket classification.

Must-have

Broken or missing inside marketing's lane — pixel not firing, no UTM convention, no closed loop.

Nice-to-have

Improvable, would help but not urgent. Better A/B testing, ad-creative versioning.

Brand-protected

GM has a strong preference. Document, observe, leave alone unless it blocks measurement.

Leave-alone

Outside marketing scope: CSR scripts, dispatch logic, technician quoting. Observe only.

The Political Innovation

When a GM sees their long-standing preference flagged as brand-protected instead of as a standardization target, the conversation shifts from defense to collaboration.

GMs start telling me which preferences are real and which are habits they wouldn't mind losing.

"My job is to make the trustworthy data layer; it's the GMs' job to decide what to do with it. I make the case, they make the call."

Section 11 · Risks

Five risks I expect — and the surface they break on first.

Other GMs feel deprioritized while the pilot runs
Mitigation: Every brand still gets Phase 1 baseline visibility from day 30. Roadmap names the cascade order.
Wrong pilot brand picked
Mitigation: Commonality Matrix is the explicit input. Pilot Selection Memo at day 30 has CMO + Pilot GM + IT sign-off.
ServiceTitan data quality gaps
Mitigation: Phase 1 audit surfaces them. Blank Job Tag fields become a Phase 2 workstream — not a Phase 3 surprise.
Pilot succeeds but doesn't replicate cleanly
Mitigation: Playbook is written during Phase 2. Brand 2 added in Phase 3 specifically to stress-test it.
Dashboard gets ignored
Mitigation: Friday Growth Review from Week 6. Every metric has a named decision owner. If nobody owns it, it gets cut.
Scope creep into operations or creative
Mitigation: Charter scope boundaries — observed-only for CSR, dispatch, field ops. I propose only inside marketing's lane.
